About

Research interests and background

My work sits at the meeting point of mathematical modelling and scientific computing. I derive models for heat transfer in physical and biological media, solve them numerically using finite difference methods, and implement the solvers in Python so that the results can be reproduced and reused.

My current research concerns bioheat transfer: the modelling of temperature in living tissue, where blood perfusion and metabolic heat generation act alongside conduction. This has direct application to magnetic hyperthermia, in which nanoparticles are used to heat a tumour into a therapeutic temperature range while sparing the tissue around it.

Alongside research, I write and teach. I believe numerical methods are best learned by deriving them, implementing them, and then testing whether the implementation behaves as the mathematics predicts, and I try to make that sequence explicit in everything I produce.

Finite Difference Methods for Solving Heat Equations with Python

From Mathematical Models to Computational Simulations

A book for readers who want to move carefully from a mathematical model to a working simulation. Each method is introduced through its mathematical structure, developed into an algorithm, implemented in Python, and then verified against something known to be true.

The emphasis throughout is on understanding rather than recipes: why a scheme is stable, what a truncation error means in practice, and how to tell whether a program is solving the equation you intended.

Contents

  1. Introduction to Heat Equations
  2. The Explicit Method
  3. The Implicit Method
  4. The Crank–Nicolson Method
  5. Heat Conduction in Cylindrical and Spherical Coordinates
  6. Nonlinear Bioheat Transfer

Further chapters on two-dimensional problems, thermal damage modelling, and efficient solvers are in preparation. If you would like to be told when the book is available, please write to me at the address below.

Research

Current areas of work

Bioheat Transfer

Pennes bioheat modelling with temperature-dependent blood perfusion, metabolic heat generation, and layered tissue geometries.

Magnetic Hyperthermia

Modelling of nanoparticle heating in tumour tissue, therapeutic temperature windows, and the sensitivity of treatment to perfusion.

Finite Difference Methods

Explicit, implicit, and Crank–Nicolson schemes; stability and convergence analysis; nonlinear problems solved by Newton iteration.

Scientific Python

Reproducible numerical software for heat transfer problems, with verification built into the workflow rather than added afterwards.

Teaching

Courses and materials

I have taught applied and computational mathematics at university level, and I currently tutor mathematics on a voluntary basis at Rochester Community and Technical College. I write teaching material that pairs each method with a working program. Topics I regularly cover include:

  • Numerical solution of ordinary and partial differential equations
  • Finite difference methods and their stability
  • Scientific programming with Python and NumPy
  • Mathematical modelling of heat and mass transfer

Notes and example programs will be published here as they are prepared.
